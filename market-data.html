Why this exists

Rare whisky is a market. Markets need data.

Headline prices for rare Scotch — a Macallan 1926 at £2.1 million, a Yamazaki 50 at close to a million euros — make the news. But the day-to-day market is quieter: thousands of bottles changing hands at auction every month, and a UK secondary market worth tens of millions a year.

This page tracks the figures that matter: the record bottles, the latest auction results, and the size of the market itself — all sourced from published auction-house results and Rare Whisky 101.

“The most expensive bottle is the story. The market total is the truth.”

The vault

£2,125,000. One bottle.

The Macallan 1926 remains the most expensive bottle of whisky ever sold at auction — £2,125,000 at Sotheby's in November 2023, equivalent to $2,807,975.

Who buys

Collectors, drinkers and investors.

01

Collectors

Chasing complete sets, distillery anniversaries and one-of-one lots. The rarest bottles are bought to be kept — sometimes for decades.

02

Drinkers

Older vintages and closed distilleries command a premium because they can never be made again. Every bottle opened makes the rest rarer.

03

Investors

Rare whisky has outperformed many traditional assets over the past decade — but the market is illiquid, unregulated and driven by a handful of distilleries.
